HyperSol HyperSpace 3D

An open-source desktop web browser with a 3D interface. Ordinary websites float as panels in a 3D room, page sections lift into layered depth, and fully 3D sites written in HoloML open as places you walk through. Free, Apache 2.0, no telemetry. A developer preview: experimental, not yet for everyday browsing.

The story

In early 2001 two computer engineering students who had met at Florida Atlantic University, Sunny Rajpal and Mauricio Sadicoff, formed HyperSol, a small Florida software company with a plain mission: build high-quality software that makes people's time on a computer more productive and more fun. Its first product was a web browser, HyperSol WebSurfer, born when they needed features no other browser would allow, starting with control over the pop-up windows of 2001. Version 1.0.0 shipped on March 5, 2001, free, with themes that changed the whole browsing environment, automatic page refresh, and a full HTML editor inside the browser.

Behind the shipped features sat a bigger idea: a browser where the web itself is not flat, sites rendered in three dimensions, a browser you look into, not at. Between 2001 and 2003 that idea became the concept for HyperSol's next step, HyperSpace 3D. An early concept screen survives, a blue cube tilted toward the viewer over a loading bar. It stayed a concept of its time, with no claim that it shipped as a product: the hardware, the graphics APIs, and the open web platform were not ready.

Twenty-five years later they are. HyperSol HyperSpace 3D marks the 25th anniversary of HyperSol's formation by finally building that idea, in the open, for everyone, built through agentic coding: AI coding agents directed by one person who kept the scope, the decisions, the review, and the acceptance, with every prompt published. HyperSol the company no longer exists; this is a personal project that honours it, not a commercial product. The whole of it, from the first prompt, is in the repository's README.

HoloML: fully 3D websites

HoloML is a markup language for 3D websites, written the way a page is and kept in its own repository, with its specification, a parser and a checker, and an extension for VS Code; its documentation is at srajpal.github.io/holoml. HyperSpace 3D shows a HoloML page across the whole window, to orbit or walk around. Seven example sites show what a page can do; each opens here in any browser with WebGL 2, through the viewer on a plain page, or in HyperSpace 3D from its start panel.

  • The showroom: five cars in a hall, each to walk around in three colours. Its site
  • Blockworld: a small block game; break and place blocks, and find five gems. Its site
  • The sofa studio: a shop page with shadows, textured fabrics, and choices that change the sofa in place. Its site
  • Harbour Loft: a flat by the harbour to tour, with doors, lamps, and a roof terrace. Its site
  • The sneaker store: one shoe in ten colourways to walk among and turn over. Its site
  • The ocean tunnel: an aquarium to walk through, with 30 fish. Its site
  • Words in a room: signs in three languages, two of them right to left. Its site

To write a page of your own, the HoloML playground has its text beside its scene, checked as you type, starting from a first page or any of the seven.

The viewer fetches an example only when you open it, and runs a page's scripts only when the page is on srajpal.github.io, as the examples are, so their scripts run here too; a page of another site shows here without its scripts, and in full in HyperSpace 3D itself.

Running it

Version {{version}}, a developer preview, as a portable zip: take the one for your computer, unzip it anywhere, and start it. Nothing to install, nothing to sign in to, and no update check. Nothing is signed, so each system asks once the first time: Windows SmartScreen (More info, then Run anyway), macOS Gatekeeper (System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway), and on Linux where user namespaces are restricted the chrome-sandbox helper is given its rights first; the README's Download section has each step, the checksums, and how to check each zip's build provenance attestation.

  • Windows 10 or 11, x64
  • macOS, Apple silicon
  • macOS, Intel
  • Linux, x64

Or build it from source with Node 24 or newer and pnpm (the version is pinned in the repository): 

git clone https://github.com/srajpal/hypersol-hyperspace-3d.git
cd hypersol-hyperspace-3d
pnpm install --frozen-lockfile
pnpm dev

CONTRIBUTING.md has the full setup, the tests, and how to make your own zip. Every release is on the releases page.
